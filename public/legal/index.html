Copies on your device: the app keeps copies of your readings, chart and conversations on your device so they open quickly. On a device used with more than one account, each account's copies are kept apart and are shown only to that account.

Memory: while memory is on, the Oracle keeps short, distilled memories from your conversations (one to two sentences each), recalls relevant past moments, and keeps her own notes about how your conversations have been going, so future sessions feel continuous. Each memory and note records whether it came from a conversation involving another member (Section 3.7). Memory is on by default; you can switch it off or clear it in Settings (Section 5.2).

Personality note (test): we are testing a private note the Oracle forms about your personality style from how you write, used only to shape her tone. Today it is made only for the founders' own accounts, not for any other member. Where it exists it follows the rules for memories: it is not made while memory is off, it is deleted when you clear memory, and it is deleted if it came from a conversation with a member who later leaves (Section 3.7).

People you connect with or add: your soul connections, shared group readings, and the people you save in Dynamics (their name and birth details). See Section 3.7.

Payment metadata: for web payments, the Teya card token, the card's last four digits and brand, and the record of charges; for purchases made in the apps, the App Store or Google Play transaction identifiers and purchase history needed to verify your subscription; billing status; current plan. We do not see or store full card numbers.

Product analytics: first-party usage events (for example which screens you opened and which features you used), stored with your account. They never contain chat content, birth data or anything you type. Analytics are on by default and you can switch them off in Settings (Section 5.3). Events are deleted automatically after 90 days.

Quality records: to measure and improve the Oracle, we keep two kinds of record. For each Oracle reply in chat, a technical record of how it was made (which model wrote it, which of your memories and past moments were used, how long it took), with an excerpt of your message (up to about 1,000 characters) and of her reply (up to about 2,000 characters). And when OpenAI checks a reply (Section 6), its quality score, with excerpts of the same length. Only Solray's team can see them. They are kept until you delete your account; deleting a conversation does not delete them. Messages answered with extra care or crisis support keep no excerpt. Excerpts from a conversation that included another member's chart are deleted when that person deletes their account or your connection ends (Section 3.7).

Notifications: if you allow notifications on your iPhone, the device's push token, so we can send your daily note (Section 5.5).

Safety check: if you have agreed to AI processing, each message you send to the Oracle is read by an AI safety check to decide whether to show you crisis support (Section 3.6). We keep no separate copy of your message for this. We keep only: a mark on the conversation, with the time, when crisis support was shown in it, so the Oracle stays gentle there (deleted with the conversation or your account); for a voice message, the outcome, tied to a one-way code made from the transcript and not to its text, so the same outcome holds when the message is sent (it counts for 30 minutes and is removed in a later clean-up); and a count of how many checks ran for you that day.

Voice: if you use the voice composer, your recording is sent to a transcription provider (Section 6) to turn it into text. We do not keep the recording; the resulting text goes into your message like anything you type.

3.6 Messages that suggest risk

Every message to the Oracle, including your own messages in group readings and voice messages once transcribed, is checked for signs of a risk of suicide or self-harm before the Oracle writes anything. This happens whatever your plan, so crisis support never depends on a subscription.

  • How it is checked. Our own servers first look for words that suggest risk, without any AI provider. If you have agreed to AI processing (Section 5.1), an AI safety check also reads your whole message to decide whether to show you crisis support. With it go, where they can be, a few of your own earlier messages in that conversation (never anything another person wrote, and never earlier messages that were answered with crisis support) and a note of whether you spoke of not wanting to live earlier in the conversation. The safety check uses Anthropic first and then OpenAI, with about three seconds in all (Section 6).
  • If the safety check cannot answer. If it gives no usable answer in time, or it has already read a very high number of your messages that day (currently 500), two things decide instead: the check on our own servers, and the Oracle herself. For that message, the AI writing her reply (Section 6) is also asked to look for signs that you are at risk right now. If she finds them, you see the crisis card instead of her reply, unless our own check clearly found that the words are not about you (for example, they are about someone else or are a quote). Her reply is then neither shown nor kept. If you have not agreed to AI processing, or you are under 16, no AI reads your message and the check on our own servers decides on its own.
  • What happens next. Most messages, including sad or heavy ones, get the Oracle's usual reply. When a message may carry real pain, the Oracle answers with extra care: gently, without reading the pain from your chart, and with one mention of where a real person can be reached. When a message suggests you may be at risk, the Oracle does not answer with AI. Instead you see a crisis card in your language, with helplines you can call, text or chat with at once, your own country's first (worked out from your time zone, or from your language when the time zone does not tell us; for example 112, 988 in the United States, Samaritans 116 123 in the United Kingdom, Línea 024 in Spain and 1717 in Iceland), and a link to findahelpline.com for other countries. If a message suggests immediate danger, the card puts your emergency number first. If you write that someone else may be in danger right now, the Oracle's reply ends with a line asking you to call the emergency number (your country's, where we know it) and to stay with that person if it is safe. If you are shown a crisis card yourself, the same line is added to the card. After a crisis card, the Oracle stays gentle for the rest of that conversation.
  • When there is no Oracle reply. Sometimes a message that suggests things are heavy cannot get an Oracle reply: for example you have not agreed to AI processing, you are under 16, your membership is not active, you have reached the daily limit, the message is too long, or the Oracle cannot be reached. You then see a gentler card listing the same helplines, together with the usual note.
  • What is kept. Your conversations stay private to you and include anything you wrote, even when we showed you crisis support. Once a message has been answered with crisis support, the AI never uses it again: not as history, memory or any analysis. You can delete a conversation at any time. In a group reading, a message answered with crisis support is shown only to you and is not kept in either person's copy of the reading. Messages the Oracle answered with extra care are not sent for the quality check, keep no excerpt in our quality records (Section 3.2) and are not turned into memories or recalled moments. Sensitive moments are also kept out of unprompted recall.

4. The Collective

The Collective is Solray's anonymous, shared layer: when members choose to join it, coarse parts of their charts are counted together so the Oracle can recognise patterns across many people. It is off by default. You join only by ticking the box at signup or turning it on in Settings, and you can leave at any time.

4.1 What counts in the Collective

  • Coarse chart components only (for example sun sign, Human Design type, Gene Key spheres). Never your name, raw birth data, conversations or memories.
  • Patterns computed from those components across members who have joined.
  • Patterns reach your own Oracle only while you are in the Collective yourself.

4.2 Ten-person minimum

Nothing from the Collective reaches anyone's Oracle unless at least 10 distinct members who are in the Collective right now stand behind it. Smaller groupings are never shown.

4.3 Separate from everything else

The Collective is its own switch. Joining or leaving it does not change your AI consent, memory or analytics, and those do not change it.

4.4 Leaving

When you leave, your contributions are removed at once (your chart signals and your entries in the shared patterns), and the pattern counts are recalculated, so you stop counting immediately. They are also removed when you delete your account.

4.5 Chart history

Changes to your chart components (for example when you correct a birth time) are recorded as history alongside the current chart. This history is deleted when you delete your account.

5. Your choices and switches

Each of these is separate: changing one does not change the others. Each choice is recorded with its date.

5.1 AI processing

The Oracle and written readings are made by AI providers (Section 6), so they need your permission to receive your data. We ask for it, with a link to this page, when you sign up; an account cannot be created without it. If you joined before we asked this way, the app asks you once, and you can choose "Not now": your chart, Souls and settings keep working, and the AI parts wait until you agree.

Your consent is recorded with the version of the wording you agreed to and the date. If we change which providers receive your data, or what they receive, in a way that matters, we change the version and ask you again.

You can withdraw it at any time in Settings (AI processing). From that moment nothing of yours is sent to any AI provider: the Oracle chat, your daily, weekly and long-range readings, Dynamics and compatibility readings, voice transcription, memory building, quality checks, the AI safety check and the nightly reading and its notification all stop, including any that were already under way. The check for risk on our own servers keeps working, so you still see crisis support if you need it (Section 3.6). Readings that would include you in someone else's conversation stop too. Your chart, your saved conversations and the rest of the app stay. You can agree again whenever you like.

5.2 Memory

Memory is on by default. When it is off, the Oracle speaks only from your chart and the conversation in front of her: nothing new is remembered from any conversation, and nothing she remembered before is used. "Clear memory" is a real fresh start: it deletes every memory and everything else the Oracle derived about you (recalled moments, her own notes about your conversations, breakthroughs, your growth arc, what she was anticipating, resonance moments, and the personality note in Section 3.2 where one exists), and stops any memory still being written from an earlier conversation. Clearing memory does not delete your conversations; delete those separately. Switch memory off or clear it in Settings (Memory). You can also email privacy@solray.ai from your account email and we will do it for you.

5.3 Analytics

First-party product analytics are on by default. Turn them off in Settings (Anonymous analytics) and no further events are recorded. You can also ask us to delete the events already recorded.

5.4 The Collective

Off by default; on only if you choose. See Section 4.

5.6 Your chart's visibility

Private by default: your soul connections see your name and photo only. Set it to Public in Settings to let them see your chart and to let it be used in readings that include you (Section 3.7).

A few billing records are kept after deletion, because accounting law requires the money record and so that a deleted account cannot be used to take a second free trial or to claim someone else's store purchase. They hold no name, email address, chart or conversation, only keyed hashes (one-way codes made with a secret key), the random internal number your account had, amounts, dates, the payment provider's or store's transaction references and, for billing items our team checked, a short note of what happened:

  • the accounting ledger of charges, refunds and refund reversals, keyed to a hash of your account number;
  • the trial record, keyed to a hash of your normalised email address, which stops a second free trial;
  • records of card charge attempts, and of billing situations our team had to check (for example a refund that reached us before the charge it refunds), whether still open or settled;
  • store purchase owners: which store purchase (Apple's original transaction number, or a hash of Google's purchase token) belonged to which account number, so that purchase can never be attached to another account; and the log of store notifications received;
  • if you joined through an invite, a marker keyed to a hash of your email so the invite reward is given only once.

Deleting your account does not cancel an App Store or Google Play subscription; cancel it in the store. If a store subscription goes on renewing after you delete your account, we keep receiving the store's notices about it and may check the purchase with the store. Each renewal the store confirms, and any refund or reversal of a refund, is added to the same anonymised ledger, keyed only to the hash of your old account number. It is not attached to any account and gives no access.
